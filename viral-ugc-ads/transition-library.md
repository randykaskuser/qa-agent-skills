# Transition & Visual Pacing Library

Transitions maintain the viewer's attention by constantly resetting their visual cortex. A static shot longer than 3 seconds kills retention.

## Google Flow Prompting for Transitions
Google Flow doesn't inherently understand "jump cut." You must describe the *effect* of the transition in the camera movement or action.

## 1. The Whip Pan
- **What it is**: A fast blur as the camera moves rapidly on the X-axis.
- **How to prompt it**: "Camera whip pans left to reveal [New Subject]."
- **When to use**: Moving from Scene 2 (Pain) to Scene 3 (The Enemy) or changing locations.

## 2. The Match Cut
- **What it is**: Cutting between two different scenes where the composition (or actor's position) is identical.
- **How to prompt it**: "Match cut to actor in exact same position, but now in a brightly lit office."
- **When to use**: Showing Before/After (Scene 5) or Future Pacing (Scene 6).

## 3. The Smash Zoom (Crash Zoom)
- **What it is**: An aggressive, sudden zoom into the subject's face or an object.
- **How to prompt it**: "Sudden smash zoom into the actor's eyes."
- **When to use**: Emphasizing a shocking statistic or a painful truth (Scene 2 or 3).

## 4. The Screen Push
- **What it is**: Moving physically closer to a screen to transition into a screen recording.
- **How to prompt it**: "Camera pushes rapidly into the laptop screen, transitioning fully into the UI."
- **When to use**: Introducing the Mechanism / Software (Scene 4).

## 5. The Drop / Throw
- **What it is**: Using an object falling out of frame or hitting the table to motivate a cut.
- **How to prompt it**: "Actor drops a stack of papers on the desk. On impact, cut to..."
- **When to use**: The Pattern Interrupt (Scene 1) or dismissing the "old way" (Scene 3).

## 6. The UI Pop
- **What it is**: Overlays appearing with accompanying sound design (implied).
- **How to prompt it**: "Three UI notification bubbles pop onto the screen in rapid succession."
- **When to use**: Social Proof (Scene 5) or escalating Pain (Scene 2).

## The 3-Second Rule
If a scene is listed as 6 seconds long, you MUST include a visual change at the 3-second mark.
**Example**:
- 0-3s: Medium shot of actor speaking.
- 3-6s: Zoom in slightly, overlay text appears on the left side of the screen.
